# Port Avelin Visitor Guide

Port Avelin is a harbour town of about 38,000 people on the northern coast. It is known for its lighthouse, its fish market and the old salt warehouses that now hold galleries.

## Getting there

### By train

Direct trains leave the capital every two hours. The trip takes 3 hours 20 minutes and ends at Avelin Central, a ten-minute walk from the harbour.

### By ferry

From May to September a ferry runs from Skerra Island twice a day, at 08:30 and 16:00. Bicycles travel free; cars must be booked one week ahead.

## Getting around

The old town is small and mostly car-free. A day pass for the local buses costs 6 crowns. Bike rental is available at the station for 15 crowns per day.

## Things to see

### Greyhook Lighthouse

The lighthouse was built in 1871 and is 41 metres tall. You can climb its 212 steps between 10:00 and 17:00. Entry costs 8 crowns, and children under 12 go free.

### The Salt Warehouses

Six warehouses along the east quay were restored in 2009. Today they hold galleries, a ceramics studio and the Maritime Archive, which is open Tuesday to Saturday.

### Harbour Fish Market

The market opens at 05:00 and is busiest before 07:00. Most stalls close by noon. Try the smoked mackerel and the local rye flatbread.

## Food and drink

- **The Copper Kettle** serves fish stew and is cash only.
- **Lindqvist Bakery** is famous for cardamom buns; it closes on Mondays.
- **Northlight Brewery** runs tours on Fridays at 18:00.

Tap water is safe to drink. Most shops accept cards, but some market stalls take cash only.

## Seasonal events

The Lantern Festival takes place on the last weekend of August, when boats sail out with paper lanterns after dark. In December the warehouses host a winter market for three weekends.
